\begin{abstract}
Multivariate time-series imputation requires recovering local trajectories from observations whose availability varies across time and variables. The central challenge is to integrate complementary contextual and patch-level information while retaining numerical consistency with visible measurements. We introduce \Model, an evidence-guided coarse-to-fine framework for multivariate time-series imputation. The framework comprises four components: temporal--variable context encoding, multi-source patch evidence retrieval, pointwise evidence-guided reconstruction, and observation-conditioned residual refinement. Context encoding captures temporal dynamics and cross-variable dependencies. Patch retrieval organizes current-window and training-history fragments into a common evidence bank, which is read separately at each target point to augment a contextual base estimate. Residual refinement subsequently combines this estimate with visible observations and observation-derived references to correct reconstruction errors. Experiments cover six datasets from diverse application domains. In paired three-seed studies against a capacity-matched context-only model, the evidence pathway reduces final MSE by \GainETTMSE{} on ETTh1 and \GainPAMMSE{} on PAMAP2. Component, missing-condition and efficiency analyses further characterize how patch evidence and observation-conditioned refinement contribute to recovery.
\end{abstract}
