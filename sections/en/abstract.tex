\begin{abstract}
Time-series imputation supports reliable analysis in energy, environmental monitoring, finance, healthcare and wearable sensing. Existing recurrent, attention-based and diffusion models can reconstruct overall trajectories, yet their imputed values may still deviate substantially from the true values when observations are sparse or consecutive entries are missing. To address this problem, we introduce \Model, a general reconstruction--refinement framework that unifies point--patch evidence reconstruction with observation-guided residual correction. Specifically, \Model{} comprises four components: (1) Point-Level Context Encoding (PCE), (2) Multi-Source Patch Evidence Construction (MPEC), (3) Pointwise Evidence-Guided Reconstruction (PEGR), and (4) Observation-Guided Residual Refinement (OGRR). PCE extracts temporal and cross-variable context, MPEC organizes temporal, cross-variable and training-history patches, and PEGR integrates point-level context with patch evidence to form an initial reconstruction. OGRR subsequently combines a temporal-context-driven neural residual with an observation-reference-guided gated correction, improving recovery at missing positions while preserving visible measurements. Experiments on six datasets across diverse domains and four missingness configurations demonstrate consistently competitive performance, with best or second-best results in multiple settings. OGRR also improves other imputation backbones while keeping their weights frozen and demonstrates strong standalone recovery without a preceding learned reconstruction network. Our work establishes the value of combining point--patch evidence reconstruction with observation-guided correction, offering a path toward reusable time-series recovery models across domains.
\end{abstract}
